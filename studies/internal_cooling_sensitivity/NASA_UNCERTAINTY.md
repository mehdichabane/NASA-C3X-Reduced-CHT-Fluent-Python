# NASA-informed interpretation of the internal-cooling sensitivity

## Scope

NASA-CR-168015 reports an estimated `+/-3%` uncertainty for the radial
cooling-hole heat-transfer-coefficient calculation used in the experimental
thermal reduction. This note maps that reported magnitude onto the existing
internal-cooling `h` sensitivity family.

The result is deliberately called a **NASA-informed common-mode sensitivity
envelope**. It is not a probability interval, a confidence interval or a formal
CFD input-uncertainty distribution. NASA does not provide a probability model or
coverage level that would justify those stronger interpretations.

The primary report is Hylton et al., *Analytical and Experimental Evaluation of
the Heat Transfer Distribution over the Surfaces of Turbine Vanes*,
NASA-CR-168015 (1983):
https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19830020105

The repository transcription of the relevant uncertainty record is
[`references/experimental_data/c3x_experimental_uncertainty_summary.csv`](../../references/experimental_data/c3x_experimental_uncertainty_summary.csv).
The source-to-model decisions are listed in
[`nasa_uncertainty_mapping.csv`](nasa_uncertainty_mapping.csv).

## Calculation

The completed internal-cooling study already contains the central cases
`h/h0 = 0.95`, `1.00` and `1.05`. For each response `Y`, the local sensitivity is
computed from the symmetric pair as

```text
dY / d(+1% h) = [Y(1.05 h0) - Y(0.95 h0)] / 10
```

and the NASA-reported magnitude is applied around the accepted baseline as

```text
Y(h0 +/- 3%) = Y(h0) +/- 3 * dY/d(+1% h)
```

This interpolation is supported by the existing five-point screening: the
internal-HTC family is close to linear over `h/h0 = 0.90 ... 1.10`, with linear
`R2` values around `0.999` for the principal thermal responses. The calculation
is regenerated by
[`scripts/postprocess/build_nasa_informed_cooling_envelope.py`](../../scripts/postprocess/build_nasa_informed_cooling_envelope.py)
and stored in
[`nasa_informed_internal_htc_envelope.csv`](nasa_informed_internal_htc_envelope.csv).

## Result

| Quantity | Baseline | `h - 3%` | `h + 3%` | Envelope half-width |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mean external wall temperature | `655.619 K` | `657.354 K` | `653.884 K` | `1.735 K` |
| External heat-transfer rate | `35.820 kW/m` | `35.267 kW/m` | `36.372 kW/m` | `0.552 kW/m` |
| Wall-temperature bias, pressure side | `+8.887 K` | `+10.947 K` | `+6.827 K` | `2.060 K` |
| Wall-temperature bias, suction side | `+12.999 K` | `+14.582 K` | `+11.416 K` | `1.583 K` |
| Wall-temperature MAPE, pressure side | `1.448%` | `1.760%` | `1.136%` | `0.312` percentage point |
| Wall-temperature MAPE, suction side | `2.005%` | `2.243%` | `1.766%` | `0.238` percentage point |

Within this coherent `+/-3% h` interpretation, the positive SST wall-temperature
bias remains positive on both surfaces. The reported internal-HTC calculation
uncertainty therefore cannot, by itself, remove the archived SST/NASA
wall-temperature discrepancy.

This does **not** identify the cause of the remaining bias. Wall temperature also
depends on external convection, solid conduction, gas-side inputs, the retained
coolant-temperature boundary values and other model-form choices.

## What is not propagated

The `Tbulk +/-5 K` and `+/-10 K` families remain deterministic screening cases.
The retained NASA uncertainty record does not provide a quantitative uncertainty
for the passage coolant temperatures used by this reduced model, so no
probability or NASA-derived uncertainty status is assigned to those perturbations.

NASA also reports uncertainties for vane thermal conductivity, gas temperature,
pressure, geometry and test parameters. They are retained as source metadata but
are not propagated here because the present repository does not contain matching
solved sensitivity families for those inputs.

## External HTC and double counting

The `+/-3%` internal-HTC calculation term also belongs to the experimental
thermal-reduction uncertainty chain used to obtain external HTC. The repository
therefore does not add this `+/-3%` term independently to the NASA Table VI
external-HTC uncertainty intervals. Those Table VI intervals remain the
experimental error bars used in the NASA HTC comparison.

Rows for external-HTC MAPE are retained in the generated envelope only to show
the CFD response to the prescribed internal `h`; their `validation_use` field is
explicitly marked `sensitivity_only_do_not_combine_with_table_vi`.

## Status

This addition strengthens interpretation of an already completed sensitivity
campaign. It does not claim a complete ASME V&V 20 validation-uncertainty budget
and requires no new Fluent solve.
